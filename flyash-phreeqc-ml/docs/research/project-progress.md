## What the carbonate experiments showed

- **Equilibrium across temperatures and solution conditions.** Four studies supplied 110 published stock or endpoint records, including replicates and observations from shared preparations. Comparisons and controlled database variations examined the effects of temperature equations, dissolved calcium species, measured pH and CO₂ pressure. The results varied with the conditions. In a separate Loos experiment at 25 °C and a measured CO₂ partial pressure of 6.181 kPa, the MINTEQ-based calculation gave 3.259 mmol calcium per kg water against 3.297 measured, and pH 6.79 against 6.83. At 97.099 kPa its calcium prediction was 8.24% high. [Ferreira](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jced.4c00670) · [Guidone](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5RA05547D) · [Coto](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fluid.2012.03.020) · [Dorange](https://doi.org/10.7202/705074ar) · [Loos, Tables 5.5 and 7.1](https://duepublico2.uni-due.de/servlets/MCRFileNodeServlet/duepublico_derivate_00005481/loosdiss.pdf)
- **Restart and dosing behavior.** Saving the complete aqueous state at 17 significant digits restored the uninterrupted endpoint byte for byte for one tested restart. Trace redox results remained sensitive to how the dose was split, even in an uninterrupted run. In a separate finite-stock simulation, the controller delivered three 22.5 µmol carbonate pulses and withheld the fourth when stock reached zero. When a subsequent simulation ran for the duration of a published experiment, it called for 1,822.5 µmol versus 819 µmol actually delivered. The dosing checks verified the controller logic. The retrospective comparison showed that the chosen rate and dosing assumptions overestimated experimental demand. [Uchikawa et al., control experiment](https://www.soest.hawaii.edu/oceanography/faculty/zeebe_files/Publications/UchikawaGCA23.pdf)
- **Independent test of recovered calcite.** We fixed the PHREEQC equilibrium model and recorded the predictions before scoring the paired liquid and solid measurements from 29 sealed bottles. The model overpredicted recovered calcite in 28. For four dilute bicarbonate bottles, the mean final dissolved inorganic carbon was 4.302 mmol/kg predicted and 4.338 measured, while the predicted 69.8 mg of calcite per bottle exceeded the 10.5 mg recovered. This limits the use of equilibrium alone to predict how much solid an experiment will collect in a given time. [Randazzo, chapter 2 and Table 2.5](https://prod-ms-be.lib.mcmaster.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/545ef2cc-ea1a-420c-8b8c-dd96fe5f5ec2/content)

## Mixture calculations and next independent tests

The research preview calculates starting elemental inventories from dry masses and assays, accounts for blanks, dilution and removed samples in solution recoveries, and runs selected mineral equilibria for specified materials. For example, the published assays of a blend with 80% fly ash and 20% red mud imply about 119 g aluminium, 93 g iron and 180 g silicon per kilogram of dry blend. Predicting how much of that inventory dissolves requires independent filtrate measurements under matching conditions. [Koshy et al., Tables 1 and 2](https://doi.org/10.3390/cryst9110572) · [Leaching validation and release criteria](leaching-progress.md)

For precipitation, the next decisive comparison needs known starting alkalinity and carbon input, time-resolved solution measurements, and independently weighed, identified calcite. For scandium leaching, it needs three qualifying two-hour citrate treatments on a new untreated red-mud lot, each linked to a filtered-liquid scandium assay. In both cases, record all eligible predictions before examining the external outcomes.
