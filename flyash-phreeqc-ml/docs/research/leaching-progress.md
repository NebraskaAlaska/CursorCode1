# Leaching progress

**22 November 2026.** The project can calculate metal recovery when a laboratory supplies measurements of the starting solid and the liquid. In one red-mud batch, recipe conditions helped predict dissolved scandium. In tests on different materials, calcium and aluminium forecasts missed by more than a simple average. The available experiments do not establish predictive accuracy for an unfamiliar red mud, fly ash or blend.

## What was tested

### Can the calculator reproduce a laboratory result?

Ray, Tripathy and Mishra measured scandium, lanthanum, cerium, praseodymium, neodymium and samarium in an Indian red-mud sample and in liquid after leaching with citric acid. We summed the six liquid concentrations, divided by the sum in the digested starting sample, and multiplied by 100, following the authors’ equal-volume convention. The calculation reproduced all 20 published combined recoveries within **0.00472 percentage points**. For example, it returned 90.164% where the paper reported 90.16%. This checks the calculation of a combined result. It gives no individual metal a validated predictive yield. For real samples we need actual liquid volumes, dilution, any liquid removed for sampling and background metal. [Ray et al., Tables 1 and 4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-17134-7) · [Reproduce the calculation offline](evidence/README.md)

### Can the starting recipe predict dissolved scandium?

We grouped Ray’s 20 runs into 15 distinct recipes, keeping repeats together. Each time we set one recipe aside, fitted a simple equation to the remaining 14 using acid strength, temperature and contact time, and predicted the omitted recipe. A second calculation predicted the mean of those same 14 recipes as a baseline. We chose the approach after seeing the published table, so this is a retrospective test within one material at 50 mL of starting liquid per gram of dry solid. It is an empirical calculation, separate from PHREEQC. [Ray et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-17134-7)

The model’s **mean absolute error was 0.295 mg/L**, compared with **0.991 mg/L** for the baseline. Put plainly, its prediction typically missed the laboratory reading by about three-tenths of a milligram per litre, counting misses in either direction. The mean absolute relative error was 14.30%. The largest miss was 0.771 mg/L, equivalent to 15.17 percentage points of the starting scandium inventory on the nominal-volume basis. That is above our 15-point limit for a future external test.

We also tried transferring a reported scandium recovery of 88.79% from another laboratory to the Indian feed. Only four of 20 runs came within 20% of measured concentration. All 20 differed from the original recipe in at least one condition. This illustrates the error from borrowing a single recovery percentage across materials and conditions. [Shan et al.](https://doi.org/10.11785/S1000-4343.20240611) · [Ray et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-17134-7)

A published 80% fly ash / 20% red-mud blend, for example, contains about **119 g aluminium, 93 g iron and 180 g silicon per kg of dry blend** on the supplied oxide analyses. These are starting quantities. How much enters the liquid depends on the minerals, reagent, time and subsequent precipitation. ## Why a second scandium study is needed

For a model that also varies the liquid-to-solid ratio, Gentzmann and colleagues supplied 27 recovery measurements at 18 distinct settings on Hungarian red mud. Reported scandium recovery spans 4–35%. At 55 °C and the same reported acid strength (2 N), the mean rose from 18.5% at 6.59 mL/g to 26.3% at 15 mL/g. The two-hour duration is given for the experimental design. The table does not repeat it for every row. We retain the reported acid units because a conversion to mol/L depends on the preparation. These are development observations. The variable-loading predictor is still unfitted. [Gentzmann et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gexplo.2022.107041) · [Supplementary Tables A5, B1 and B3](https://ars.els-cdn.com/content/image/1-s2.0-S0375674222000991-mmc1.docx)

Two archives seemed promising. Li’s workbook contains two liquid tables with 30 and 28 scandium entries during **pH-controlled precipitation**. Neither table supplies the starting recipes for three two-hour citrate leaches. The Byers index contains 305 distinct study IDs. Thirteen were tagged as red mud, and one additional study examined slag made from red mud. Following those references yielded no eligible independent series. Other records remain unresolved where the public methods or individual scandium measurements are missing. [Li workbook](https://zenodo.org/records/22986614) · [Byers index](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/y9dbgc6th7/2) · [Source-by-source screening decisions](evidence/scandium-source-screen.md)

## Plan from development to release

1. **Define reliability — complete.** On an independent lot, mean absolute relative error in dissolved scandium must be at most 20%, mean absolute recovery error at most 10 percentage points, and the largest absolute recovery error at most 15 points. The candidate must beat the development-average baseline on mean absolute error for both outputs. If measurement uncertainty could reverse the decision, the result is inconclusive.
2. **Assemble development evidence — complete for screening.** We recorded the fixed-loading Indian recipes, the Hungarian variable-loading series, repeat measurements, assay units and limitations. These are separate models and datasets.
3. **Reserve an independent experiment — current step.** Identify a different lab with one untreated red-mud lot and at least three distinct, two-hour leaches using citric acid and water, inside the joint range of the development recipes. Obtain sample IDs, feed scandium, dry mass, reagent preparation, temperature, initial and final liquid volumes, and confirmation of filtered-liquid Sc assays, dilution, sampling losses and analytical uncertainty. Keep numerical outcomes hidden while checking eligibility.
4. **Develop and freeze.** Reserve the whole lot and all eligible rows before fitting. Use only the development data to choose the candidate and baseline. Save both and every external prediction before opening the measurements.
5. **Score every eligible recipe.** Report predicted and measured concentrations, recovery, mass balance, uncertainty and comparison with the baseline. Disclose prior exposure to any published outcomes.
6. **Release within the demonstrated range.** A pass on one independent lot supports that lot and the tested recipes. Broader claims about other elements, fly ashes or blends require their own independent evidence.
